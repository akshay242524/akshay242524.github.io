ABOUT ME

The work I'm proudest of sits where firmware and machine learning meet. I trained PyTorch and XGBoost models on modem trace logs to make smarter scheduling and power decisions, then got them running on resource-constrained ARM hardware, which improved on-device responsiveness by about 52%. Getting from a model that works on a laptop to one that behaves on the device is its own kind of debugging, and it's the part I enjoy most.

Most of those projects started as a conversation with hardware, firmware, and use-case teams about a performance problem nobody had a clean answer for, and turning it into something a model could actually learn from. I also looked after the lab side, writing Python and MATLAB tools that automated firmware flashing, RF measurements, and trace collection, which gave everyone back about a third of their manual lab time each bring-up cycle. Along the way I learned that a feature isn't done when it compiles. It's done when it lands cleanly alongside everyone else's work on a shared release date.

After MSD, I went back to school and earned my Master's in Computer Science at UNC Charlotte with a 4.0 GPA. I'm now a Research Associate there, building an offline Android app with an on-device OpenCV pipeline that has to give the same answer on very different phones. Outside of that, I keep an eye on where wireless and AI are heading, and on the places where the two are starting to overlap.
