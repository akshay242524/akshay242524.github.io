ABOUT ME

Most of my work has lived in the layer between the radio and the rest of the network. At MSD, I owned features in our access point software from the first design conversation all the way to production, writing C and C++ on embedded Linux. Some weeks that meant chasing TCP/IP failures and broadcast storms through packet captures and kernel traces. Other weeks it was writing the channel and power arbitration logic that stopped neighboring access points from stepping on each other, or building a small monitoring service in C so we could actually see CPU, memory, and radio usage on the devices themselves.

I worked with teams in different time zones on shared firmware release dates, and that taught me something I still think about: a feature isn't done when it compiles. It's done when it lands cleanly alongside everyone else's work. I like owning a problem and seeing it through, and I'm just as happy to ask a teammate when they've already solved half of it.

After MSD, I went back to school and earned my Master's in Computer Science at UNC Charlotte with a 4.0 GPA. I'm now a Research Associate there, building an offline Android app with an on-device OpenCV pipeline that has to give the same answer on very different phones. Outside of that, I keep an eye on where wireless and AI are heading, and on the places where the two are starting to overlap.
