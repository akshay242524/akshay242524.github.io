ABOUT ME

The problems I enjoy most are the stubborn ones. On our access points, that meant TCP/IP failures and L2 broadcast storms that had already survived a few rounds of manual debugging, so I got comfortable living in packet captures and kernel traces until the root cause finally showed itself. I also wrote the channel and power arbitration logic that kept neighboring access points from stepping on each other in dense offices, and built a small monitoring service in C so we could see CPU, memory, and radio usage on the devices themselves.

I usually owned a feature from the first design conversation all the way to production, and I worked with teams in different time zones on shared firmware release dates. That taught me something I still think about: a feature isn't done when it compiles. It's done when it lands cleanly alongside everyone else's work. I like seeing a problem through, and I'm just as happy to ask a teammate when they've already solved half of it.

After MSD, I went back to school and earned my Master's in Computer Science at UNC Charlotte with a 4.0 GPA. I'm now a Research Associate there, building an offline Android app with an on-device OpenCV pipeline that has to give the same answer on very different phones. Outside of that, I keep an eye on where wireless and AI are heading, and on the places where the two are starting to overlap.
